Does your model survive analog silicon?

A local-only toolchain that answers, for your PyTorch model on your accelerator profile: which layers break under noise and quantization, where analog stops beating digital, and what recovers the accuracy.

The problem

Analog accelerators are fast and cheap — until your model says otherwise

Analog in-memory compute (PCM, ReRAM, SRAM crossbars) and coherent photonic meshes promise large energy wins on matrix math. But they inject noise, clamp precision to a handful of effective bits, and pay a steep price at every analog/digital boundary. Whether a given network actually wins is a per-model, per-profile question — and device simulators answer it from the chip's point of view, not the model's.

Noise & drift

Output noise, conductance drift and limited ENOB ruin some layers and leave others untouched. The value is knowing which — the report ranks fragile layers and shows a retention curve (fidelity after a day / a year of drift).

The converter tax

ADC/DAC and DRAM movement often dominate energy — up to ~10× the analog compute. Cheap MACs don't help if conversions eat the budget.

The break-even

There is a frontier where analog stops being worth it. Everyone can inject noise; the differentiated answer is where that line falls for you.

How it works

Instrument, cost-model, find the break-even, hand back a report

No graph capture, no model surgery you can't audit. A recursive walker swaps nn.Linear, nn.Conv2d and attention projections for noisy equivalents — the same pattern aihwkit's convert_to_analog uses — so any architecture is cheap to instrument and every step stays inspectable.

Sweeps & recovery

How hard does noise bite — and what claws it back?

A degradation curve is the mean cosine fidelity of a layer's output against its noiseless baseline, swept over a noise (σ), weight-bit, programming-noise or ADC-bit budget and averaged over seeded draws. ADC readout is reported as a bracket: a best-case auto-ranged number next to a per-tensor fixed-range one where outlier activations saturate. Per-layer quant-scale calibration then recovers fidelity by spending precision where the weights actually live instead of on outliers — never worse than the naive range at σ=0.

Illustrative shape; real curves come from analog-ready sweep.

Recovery, framed honestly

The tool reports calibrated fidelity against the same cosine metric it calibrates on — so "never worse than naive at σ=0" is a real guarantee, not a fixture artifact. The naming stays honest as the evidence grows:

Analog Robustness CI.

Simulated noise, no hardware — what you can run today.

Profile-Conditioned QAT.

Vendor-characterised profiles drive the recovery recipe.

Calibration / QAT Runtime.

Only once measured-device data backs the word "calibration".

Trust surface

Runs behind your firewall. Share only a redacted envelope.

A hardware vendor's noise/precision/energy profile is crown-jewel IP. The whole design is built so they never have to hand it over: the container runs locally, redaction lives in the data model, and doctor makes the posture auditable.

Per-field redaction in the data model

Every profile field declares how it may leave the tool — exact_ok (verbatim), bucket (coarsened to an order-of-magnitude band), or hidden (never emitted, even via repr). The shareable report carries the qualitative verdicts; a hidden coefficient like mem_energy_pj_per_byte simply never appears.

exact_ok → 8 bucket → 1..10 hidden → ∅
The surface

One CLI, nine verbs

Everything the report shows is reproducible from the command line — pure-PyTorch defaults, with network-free core commands. The real ResNet-18 path is the documented exception because torchvision may download weights.

doctor

Audit the local-only posture and backend availability.

analyze

Per-op cost, score and break-even, written to a self-contained HTML/JSON report.

sweep

A degradation curve over output σ, weight-bits, faithful weight-programming noise (prog_sigma), or ADC readout precision (adc_bits), seeded and reproducible.

demo photonic-mzi

The pure-PyTorch Clements mesh demo — zero third-party deps.

eval

Real task accuracy on a labelled set — clean vs under an illustrative noise stress.

summarize

Emit the deterministic run-summary record consumed by the CI gate.

regress

The CI primitive: gate a run against a baseline, fail closed, emit a redacted envelope.

validate

One-command, byte-deterministic validation curve plus a recovery recipe.

pilot

Run the stored measured-silicon references through the composite — an honest indicative check (the A0 baseline reproduction is still pending), never a claimed validated pass.

$ analog-ready analyze --model gpt2_block --profile aimc_pcm_4bit \
      --out report.html --redact --local-only
  → wrote report.html (per-op break-even; no hidden coefficient leaked)

$ analog-ready summarize --model mlp --profile aimc_pcm_4bit --out baseline.json
  → wrote baseline.json (deterministic CI run-summary)

$ analog-ready regress --baseline prev.json --current now.json --redact
  PASS # favorability & fidelity within tolerance; numeric deltas omitted when redacted
Honesty

Limits of this estimate

The credibility of the whole thing depends on never overclaiming. v0 is explicit about what it is and is not.

What it is

  • Directional, relative energy and favorability claims, with uncertainty bands.
  • Architecture-broad instrumentation via module replacement — reported coverage, not "100%".
  • A conservative precision envelope — "fails the envelope under this profile", not "numerically wrong".

What it is not (yet)

  • No measured-silicon calibration; coefficients are literature defaults.
  • No absolute "analog is N× better" claim — only the break-even frontier.
  • No mass/area model; a single-pass DRAM lower bound; CPU tiles.
  • Drift applies to the signed weight, not a G⁺−G⁻ differential pair — it understates drift of near-zero weights; ADC quantises per-sample, not per output column.
